NOD Findings & Conditions Unsupported by Evidence

Merged Analysis — ZA NOD (April 14, 2026) and CPC NOD (July 1, 2026)

This chart merges challenges to both Notices of Decision in the record. The ZA’s NOD (Exhibit 1, Packet Pg. 970) is the original April 14, 2026 approval. The CPC’s NOD (Exhibit 2) is the July 1, 2026 decision upholding the ZA on appeal. The BCC appeal (ZCOA2026-0013) challenges the CPC’s July 1 decision.

A critical structural difference between the two NODs: The ZA’s Finding 3 explicitly found that the site “can be developed in such a manner that undue traffic congestion or hazards will not be created” — the exact language Section 24 requires. The CPC NOD did not repeat that express language. Instead, CPC Finding 4 made a narrower ingress/egress statement and deferred the project-specific TIA and possible mitigation to building permit. The appeal argues that the available record therefore did not support the traffic determination required at CUP approval. Neither the ZA nor the CPC had a completed Traffic Impact Study when these findings were made.

Another critical difference: The ZA imposed only THREE conditions (construction permits + building footprint, impervious surface coordination, and no outdoor amplified sound). The CPC carried forward all three and expanded to 15 conditions, adding 12 more — several of which were imposed verbally during deliberations without corresponding stamped drawings in the record.

Findings

ZA Finding 1 / CPC Finding 1 “church and incidental facilities”
What Was Found

“This is a request for conditional use approval to allow a church and incidental facilities.”

What the Record Shows

Staff concluded the school “rose beyond what would be an incidental use and would be kind of a conditional use on its own” (Exhibit 11, TR-81). The Applicant’s representative then testified on the record: “remove the school entirely from the project… There is no school. There is no preschool classes. There is no elementary school. There is no middle school. There is no high school” (Exhibit 11, TR-84). Staff treated the school as removed and the CPC imposed Condition 1 prohibiting it.

The remaining issue is whether the revised scope was sufficiently definite and incorporated into the approval. The Applicant’s Written Statement (Packet Pg. 1798) still describes “educational programs, daycare, social events.” The revised site plan (Exhibit 6, Sheet S-100, Packet Pg. 1121) still shows three rooms labeled “Daycare” and the parking table still calculates “School: 3 spaces per classroom x 16 = 48 spaces.” The filed appeal identifies no amended, stamped drawings that clearly remove every school/daycare feature; the revised Sheet S-100 and written materials remained the subject of the appeal’s scope-and-plan-consistency argument.

Partially supported — verbal withdrawal on the record; remaining issue is whether revised scope was sufficiently definite and incorporated into stamped plans

ZA Finding 2 / CPC Finding 3 “site adequate in size and shape”
What Was Found

“The site for the proposed use is adequate in size and shape to accommodate the use, as the property is 3.9 acres in area and provides sufficient area to accommodate site features required by ordinance. No variances were required for the request.”

What the Record Shows

3.9 acres and no variances are facts, but they do not establish adequate access, traffic capacity, drainage, fire access, parking, and operational capacity for the complete project. The updated material showed an approximately 43,000-square-foot ground-floor footprint with parking and landscaping, but staff described CUP-level plans as “pretty conceptual,” and staff and commissioners sought additional program and floor-area clarity before deciding. The Applicant’s attendance figures also shifted throughout testimony, creating unreconciled variation in the foundational number the parking calculation depends on.

Mixed record / appeal issue — some site features shown, but conceptual plans, conflicting attendance figures, and unresolved access/drainage/fire questions remained
Brief Section: IV.A
ZA Finding 3 / CPC Finding 4 “ingress and egress” and “no undue traffic congestion or hazards”
What Was Found (ZA NOD)

“The site provides sufficient ingress and egress for vehicular access and can be developed in such a manner that undue traffic congestion or hazards will not be created. Off-street parking and access improvements by the applicant are expected to occur as well as approval from Bernalillo County Public Works through the County’s building permit process.”

What Was Found (CPC NOD)

“The site provides ingress and egress for vehicular access, but the development may require traffic improvements through conditions of approval. Off-street parking and access improvements by the applicant are expected to occur as well as approval from Bernalillo County Public Works through the County’s building permit process.” — The CPC NOD did not repeat the ZA’s express “no undue traffic congestion or hazards” language. Instead, it made a narrower ingress/egress statement and deferred the project-specific TIA and possible mitigation to building permit.

What the Record Shows

This is the principal defect. The County made this finding without a completed Traffic Impact Study or identified traffic-capacity analysis, while expressly deferring the study and potential improvements to later building-permit review. County traffic engineer Julie Luna testified that the ITE Manual is “essentially the gold standard,” that Public Works “requires a traffic study for this development,” and that no project-specific traffic data was available when the CPC acted. Luna also testified that Alameda “absolutely does have a congestion issue” and that a deferred TIA “isn’t mitigating the regional issues.”

The filed appeal argues that the Applicant’s stated attendance and vehicle figures warranted project-specific traffic analysis. The July 1 transcript confirms that Public Works required a TIA for this development, that the TIA would quantify site-generated trips, and that no project-specific TIA was available when the CPC acted. Luna also testified that Alameda “absolutely does have a congestion issue” and that a deferred TIA “isn’t mitigating the regional issues.”

The site plan shows only one main entrance gate and one main exit gate serving a facility with a stated capacity of 350-400 worshippers and up to 600 on peak days. No fire marshal review, emergency access plan, or turning-radius analysis appears anywhere in the record.

The County inverted the burden Section 24 assigns. The ordinance places the burden on the Applicant to prove no undue traffic hazards before approval — not on the Appeal Coalition to disprove them after. The County required no TIA from the Applicant, approved the CUP without one, and then forced the Appeal Coalition to search the public record for the only existing corridor study (the 2020 Murphy Express study, Exhibit 19) and enter it into the record himself. The the Appeal Coalition should never have been put in that position. The Murphy study is not in the record because the County did its job; it is there because the County did not.

The burden was printed on the front page of the Applicant’s own CUP application form. The application required the Applicant to prove that undue traffic congestion or hazards would not be created and to provide a written justification addressing that criterion. The Applicant responded “See attached,” but the attached narrative provided general statements rather than project-specific traffic evidence. Any one of the following would have satisfied the burden — a traffic scoping report, a TIA, a trip-generation calculation, a traffic count, a capacity analysis, a traffic-engineer report, or a comparable analysis demonstrating that the required traffic criterion was met. The Applicant provided none of these — not even one. The Applicant also wrote in the same application: “the expected traffic will be significant.”

The Bernalillo County Public Works Traffic Analysis Chapter requires a traffic review for “non-residential development abutting and/or accessing a County or state-maintained road,” “change in land use to a higher trip generation,” and “development that requires a change in access to an arterial or collector road.” This project meets all three criteria: it is a non-residential facility accessing 2nd Street (County-maintained) and Alameda Boulevard (NMDOT), it changes from vacant land to a regional religious facility, and the access points are on arterials. The traffic review begins with a traffic scoping report — a preliminary screening the Applicant can complete themselves — but even that was not done.

The appeal argues that a future TIA is not evidence that traffic impacts were quantified at the time of the CPC decision. The County responds through Public Works testimony that a completed TIA, mitigation agreement, and required improvements are prerequisites to building permit and opening. The precise appeal question is not whether a future TIA was required, but whether the CPC had the traffic evidence Section 24 requires at the time it made the CUP finding.

Applicant burden not met — the CUP application form itself places the burden on the applicant to prove no undue traffic congestion or hazards; the applicant provided no traffic scoping, trip generation, or screening of any kind; the County made the Section 24 finding without any project-specific traffic evidence; the CPC did not repeat the ZA’s express “no undue traffic congestion or hazards” language
Brief Section: IV.A, IV.F, IV.G, IV.K, IV.N
ZA Finding 4 / CPC Finding 5 “no adverse neighborhood effect” / “compatible”
What Was Found (ZA NOD)

“Adjacent properties are of comparable uses, and the proposed use will have no adverse effect on the neighborhood or seriously conflict with the character of the area.”

What Was Found (CPC NOD)

“The area has a mix of uses and the proposed use will add to that mix.”

What the Record Shows

The NOD gives a conclusion but does not reconcile the scale of the proposal, nearby homes, rural setting, traffic, drainage, lighting, noise, and North Valley Area Plan concerns raised in the appeal. Finding 14 of the same CPC NOD admits the site is in the “Rural Area of the Comprehensive Plan.” The Rank I Comprehensive Plan’s Rural Area policy expressly favors “alternatives to urbanization” (Packet Pg. 508). The 1993 North Valley Area Plan directs the County to “limit access to Second Street, Alameda Boulevard, and Montaño Road” and retain residential zoning along these arterials. The Applicant testified the site was chosen to serve the greater Albuquerque metro and west-of-river community — a regional draw, not a local-serving use.

The appeal argues that the CPC did not make a policy-specific analysis of compatibility with the ABC Plan’s Rural Area policies and the North Valley Area Plan. The Rural Area location itself is not disputed; the dispute is whether a policy-by-policy compatibility analysis was completed.

Mixed record / appeal issue — Rural Area location not disputed; appeal argues no policy-specific ABC/North Valley compatibility analysis was completed
Brief Section: IV.H, IV.I, IV.Q
ZA Finding 6 / CPC Finding 7 “applicant intends to provide” parking, buffers, fencing
What Was Found

“The applicant intends to provide off-street parking, landscaping, buffer landscaping and fencing in compliance with the requirements of the Comprehensive Zoning Ordinance of Bernalillo County... There will be a six-foot-tall wall along the north side.”

What the Record Shows

The NOD relies on what the Applicant “intends” to provide. The Applicant described six-foot walls and buffer function, and staff accepted updated plan material. However, the finding uses future-tense “intends to provide” rather than confirming actual compliance, and final program/floor-area questions remained. The appeal argues that an intention is not proof of completed compliance or a substitute for enforceable mitigation.

Partially supported — applicant described walls/buffer and updated materials were accepted; remaining issue is whether “intends” constitutes proof of compliance
Brief Section: IV.L
ZA Finding 7 / CPC Finding 8 “Friday service could be moved”
What Was Found

“The applicant also stated that times of the main Friday service could be moved to accommodate traffic patterns.”

What the Record Shows

This was an applicant statement, not a binding condition. The approval imposed no required service time, peak-hour limit, attendance limit, vehicle limit, or mechanism to enforce the claimed mitigation. The Applicant’s testimony about moving Friday service was offered as evidence that traffic impacts would be manageable — but nothing in the conditions makes it enforceable.

Unsupported as stand-alone mitigation — applicant’s unenforceable statement cannot substitute for the required traffic finding
Brief Section: IV.G
ZA Finding 9 / CPC Finding 11 “TIA required at building permit phase”
What Was Found

“Staff from public works stated that a Traffic Impact Study would be required at the building permit phase of the project. Road improvements to offset any impacts from the project could be required.”

What the Record Shows

County traffic engineer Julie Luna testified that Public Works has a building-permit traffic process: “Bernalillo County Public Works requires a traffic study for this development, and we require it upon building permit… We would not allow the building permit to be issued without a completed traffic impact assessment… We will not issue a building permit until the traffic study is approved and the applicant has agreed to those mitigations. We wouldn’t allow the building to open until all the mitigated constructed improvements, et cetera, were completed” (Exhibit 11, TR-204–205). Director Hamm acknowledged: “there’s some subjectivity built in here, though, because we don’t at this point in time have the traffic impact analysis” (Exhibit 11, TR-209).

The County does have a later enforcement mechanism. But that mechanism operates at the building-permit stage — after CUP approval. The precise appeal question is not whether a future TIA was required, but whether the CPC had the traffic evidence Section 24 requires at the time it made the CUP finding. Luna’s testimony confirms the County has a future process; Hamm’s testimony confirms that process had not yet produced any analysis at the time of the CUP decision. A future TIA cannot retroactively supply the substantial evidence Section 24 requires at the CUP decision point.

Valid future safeguard; appealable as insufficient support for the earlier CUP traffic finding
Brief Section: IV.A, IV.F, IV.G, IV.N
ZA Finding 10 / CPC Finding 13 “conditions protect public health, safety, welfare”
What Was Found

“Conditions of approval are necessary to ensure orderly development of the site, as well as the protection of the public health, safety, and general welfare in conformity with the intended purpose of the Zoning Ordinance.”

What the Record Shows

At the time this finding was adopted, the record contained no grading and drainage plan, no hydrology report, no noise-mitigation plan (other than no outdoor speakers), no fire safety or emergency access plan, and no stormwater pollution prevention plan. The the Appeal Coalition calculated that a 1-inch rain event generates approximately 70,000-85,000 gallons of runoff (corrected per Amendment and Errata), with no detention plan anywhere in the record. Every one of these safety analyses was deferred to the building-permit stage — a non-public process that cannot supply the substantial evidence Section 24 requires at the CUP decision point.

The same defect applies to traffic safety. Condition 9 defers the Traffic Impact Study to the building-permit stage — the same future-promise problem the brief identifies for drainage. The Board could not find that conditions protect public welfare when the single most consequential safety analysis — whether a 350-600 worshipper facility on a documented failing corridor generates undue traffic hazards — had not been conducted. A deferred TIA is an admission that traffic safety was not established at the time of approval, not evidence that it was.

The ZA’s NOD imposed THREE conditions (construction permits + building footprint, impervious surface coordination, and no outdoor amplified sound). The CPC carried forward all three and expanded to 15 conditions, but most of the additions merely restate existing code requirements (parking per Section 21, landscaping per Section 19, lighting per Light Pollution Ordinance, signage per Section 12, noise per Noise Ordinance). The conditions did not require a completed grading/drainage plan or project-specific traffic analysis before the CPC decision.

Mixed record / appeal issue — some conditions existed, but traffic, fire review, and grading/drainage were later-stage matters not completed before the CPC decision
Brief Section: IV.M (runoff figure corrected in the Amendment and Errata)
CPC Finding 14 only “site is within the Rural Area of the Comprehensive Plan”
What Was Found

“The site is within the Rural Area of the Comprehensive Plan.”

What the Record Shows

Brief Section: IV.F, IV.G, IV.N

Additional Evidentiary Gaps

Documented in the brief but not tied to a single NOD finding:

  • No stamped elevation drawings verifying building height — the 26-foot A-1 zone limit was stated on the record, but no stamped architectural elevations were submitted to verify the proposed building’s actual height. (Brief § IV.I)
  • Applicant’s trip count shifted throughout testimony — the Applicant testified roughly 100 to 180 total attendees, then 140 to 150 cars, then 100 to 120 cars at peak, then a build-out to a maximum capacity of 600. The appeal argues these figures warranted project-specific traffic analysis. (Brief § IV.G)
  • Population transfer never quantified — the Applicant testified existing mosques would close and congregations transfer; no figures or analysis provided. (Brief § IV.E)
  • Balloon Fiesta concerns never addressed — AIBF’s June 25, 2026 letter independently identified the absence of a traffic study; no TIA, no seasonal analysis, no enforceable conditions were produced. (Brief § IV.P)
  • Appeal raises questions about Public Works traffic procedures — the appeal argues that traffic scoping procedures and PWTR requirements were not followed; the County’s response is that the TIA process is triggered at building permit. (Brief § IV.N)
  • Appeal disputes the legal significance of RLUIPA — the appeal argues that Director Hamm’s concern about RLUIPA liability was legally incorrect and that RLUIPA does not exempt religious institutions from neutral traffic standards. (Brief § IV.D)
  • Appeal argues continuance motions were not addressed — the appeal states that the Appeal Coalition’s timely 60-day and 120-day continuance motions were not ruled upon at the hearing. (Brief § IV.C)
  • Appeal argues extraordinary majority vote requirement applies — the appeal cites the 20% protest petition filed with over 65% of qualifying acreage within 100 feet and argues the BCC must apply the extraordinary-majority standard under Section 25 / Rule 7. (Brief § IV.R)

Bottom Line

The County could accurately establish the identity of the application, the zoning category, the site’s Rural Area location, and that other permits would still be needed. But those are not the findings that carry the approval.

The appeal’s central position is that the County did not have adequate record evidence to support the findings that actually justify CUP approval — particularly the traffic finding (ZA Finding 3 / CPC Finding 4), site adequacy (ZA Finding 2 / CPC Finding 3), neighborhood compatibility (ZA Finding 4 / CPC Finding 5), compliance intentions (ZA Finding 6 / CPC Finding 7), the unenforceable Friday service assurance (ZA Finding 7 / CPC Finding 8), the deferred TIA (ZA Finding 9 / CPC Finding 11), and the welfare-protecting conditions (ZA Finding 10 / CPC Finding 13).

The July 1 record confirms real later-stage safeguards: a TIA is required before building permit, mitigation must be agreed to and completed before opening, outdoor amplified sound is prohibited, the 26-foot A-1 height limit is enforceable, and the school is prohibited by condition. The narrower appeal question is whether the CPC had sufficient project-specific evidence at the time of the CUP decision. The ZA expressly found “no undue traffic congestion or hazards” without a TIA. The CPC did not repeat that express language; it made a narrower ingress/egress finding and deferred the TIA to building permit. The CUP application form itself placed the burden on the Applicant to prove no undue traffic congestion or hazards — the Applicant provided no traffic scoping, trip generation, or screening of any kind. The County made the Section 24 traffic finding without any project-specific traffic evidence from the Applicant.

The CPC added conditions (school removal, 60,000 SF cap, 26-foot height) during deliberations. The 26-foot height limit is supported as an A-1 zoning standard. The school removal and 60,000 SF cap were imposed verbally; the appeal argues the revised site plan and written materials do not fully reflect the school removal, and that the 60,000 SF cap was not traffic-tested. The conditions that supposedly protect public welfare include some real code requirements, but the appeal argues that grading/drainage, fire review, and project-specific traffic analysis were later-stage matters not completed before the CPC decision.

Whether that requires outright reversal or a remand for proper findings is for the deciding body, but the later Traffic Impact Study cannot retroactively supply the pre-approval evidence the NOD relied on.
