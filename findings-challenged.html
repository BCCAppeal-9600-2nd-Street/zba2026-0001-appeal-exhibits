NOD Findings & Conditions Unsupported by Evidence

Merged Analysis — ZA NOD (April 14, 2026) and CPC NOD (July 1, 2026)

This chart merges challenges to both Notices of Decision in the record. The ZA’s NOD (Exhibit 1, Packet Pg. 970) is the original April 14, 2026 approval. The CPC’s NOD (Exhibit 2) is the July 1, 2026 decision upholding the ZA on appeal. The BCC appeal (ZCOA2026-0013) challenges the CPC’s July 1 decision.

A critical structural difference between the two NODs: The ZA’s Finding 3 explicitly found that the site “can be developed in such a manner that undue traffic congestion or hazards will not be created” — the exact language Section 24 requires. The CPC then dropped that finding entirely, replacing it with a narrow ingress/egress finding (CPC Finding 4) and a TIA deferral (CPC Finding 11). Neither the ZA nor the CPC had a completed Traffic Impact Study when these findings were made.

Another critical difference: The ZA imposed only THREE conditions (construction permits + building footprint, impervious surface coordination, and no outdoor amplified sound). The CPC carried forward all three and expanded to 15 conditions, adding 12 more — several of which were imposed verbally during deliberations without corresponding stamped drawings in the record.

Findings

ZA Finding 1 / CPC Finding 1 “church and incidental facilities”
What Was Found

“This is a request for conditional use approval to allow a church and incidental facilities.”

What the Record Shows

The Applicant’s own Dear Neighbor letter (Exhibit 24) states “our proposal includes a licensed daycare and K-12 school located entirely within the main building.” The architectural drawings on record at the time of the vote — Sheets A-200 and A-300 (dated Sept 11, 2025) — still show 18 classrooms, 4 daycare rooms, and a kids’ cafeteria. Staff itself concluded the school “rose beyond what would be an incidental use and would be kind of a conditional use on its own,” requiring a separate CUP. The Applicant’s Written Statement (Packet Pg. 1798) still describes “educational programs, daycare, social events.” No amended, stamped drawings removing the school were ever submitted.

Not supported — record contradicts the finding
Brief Section: IV.J and IV.O
ZA Finding 2 / CPC Finding 3 “site adequate in size and shape”
What Was Found

“The site for the proposed use is adequate in size and shape to accommodate the use, as the property is 3.9 acres in area and provides sufficient area to accommodate site features required by ordinance. No variances were required for the request.”

What the Record Shows

3.9 acres and no variances do not establish adequate access, traffic capacity, drainage, fire access, parking, and operational capacity for the complete project. The Applicant’s own attendance figures are irreconcilable: one submission describes 350-400 attendees generating 150-200 vehicles, while another calculates 202 spaces based on 250 attendees — a 40-60% swing in the foundational attendance figure the parking calculation depends on, never reconciled or explained.

Not supported — applicant’s own submissions contradict each other; site adequacy for parking, access, drainage, and fire safety was never established
Brief Section: IV.A
ZA Finding 3 / CPC Finding 4 “ingress and egress” and “no undue traffic congestion or hazards”
What Was Found (ZA NOD)

“The site provides sufficient ingress and egress for vehicular access and can be developed in such a manner that undue traffic congestion or hazards will not be created. Off-street parking and access improvements by the applicant are expected to occur as well as approval from Bernalillo County Public Works through the County’s building permit process.”

What Was Found (CPC NOD)

“The site provides ingress and egress for vehicular access, but the development may require improvements to the second street approach as approved by Bernalillo County Public Works.” — The CPC dropped the “no undue traffic congestion or hazards” language entirely.

What the Record Shows

This is the principal defect. The County made this finding without a completed Traffic Impact Study or identified traffic-capacity analysis, while expressly deferring the study and potential improvements to later building-permit review. County traffic engineer Julia Luna testified that the ITE Manual is “essentially the gold standard,” that a TIA is required for a project of this size and type, and that there was “nothing on record” regarding project-specific traffic data. Luna also testified that Alameda “absolutely does have a congestion issue” and that a deferred TIA “is not mitigating the regional issue.”

The Applicant’s own attendance figures — even on the lowest numbers (140-150 cars = 280-300 trips) — exceed the DPM Ch. 7 §7-5(C)(2) threshold of 100 peak-hour trips. The only traffic study in the record (2020 Murphy Express, Packet Pg. 1410) documented Level of Service F at the Alameda/4th Street intersection. No codified rule or published policy authorizes blanket TIA deferral for CUPs. NMAC §18.31.6.16 requires a TIA for development on a state highway generating 100+ peak-hour trips — Alameda is a state highway. No Public Works Traffic Report (PWTR) number was ever activated.

The site plan shows only one main entrance gate and one main exit gate serving a facility with a stated capacity of 350-400 worshippers and up to 600 on peak days. No fire marshal review, emergency access plan, or turning-radius analysis appears anywhere in the record.

The County inverted the burden Section 24 assigns. The ordinance places the burden on the Applicant to prove no undue traffic hazards before approval — not on the Appellant to disprove them after. The County required no TIA from the Applicant, approved the CUP without one, and then forced the Appellant — an 85-year-old neighbor — to search the public record for the only existing corridor study (the 2020 Murphy Express study, Exhibit 19) and enter it into the record himself. The Appellant should never have been put in that position. The Murphy study is not in the record because the County did its job; it is there because the County did not.

Additionally, under Dolan v. City of Tigard (1994) and Koontz v. St. Johns River Water Mgmt. Dist. (2013), the County can only require the Applicant to fund mitigation roughly proportional to the project’s own impact — not the corridor’s cumulative regional failure. Luna herself confirmed a deferred study “is not mitigating the regional issue.” Deferral guarantees inadequate mitigation because the County’s remedial authority is constitutionally limited at the building-permit stage.

Not supported — no project-specific traffic study exists; the ZA made the Section 24 finding without evidence; the CPC failed to make the Section 24 finding at all; deferral unauthorized by written rule and constitutionally cannot supply required mitigation
Brief Section: IV.A, IV.F, IV.G, IV.K, IV.N
ZA Finding 4 / CPC Finding 5 “no adverse neighborhood effect” / “compatible”
What Was Found (ZA NOD)

“Adjacent properties are of comparable uses, and the proposed use will have no adverse effect on the neighborhood or seriously conflict with the character of the area.”

What Was Found (CPC NOD)

“The area has a mix of uses and the proposed use will add to that mix.”

What the Record Shows

The NOD gives a conclusion but does not reconcile the scale of the proposal, nearby homes, rural setting, traffic, drainage, lighting, noise, and North Valley Area Plan concerns raised in the appeal. Finding 14 of the same CPC NOD admits the site is in the “Rural Area of the Comprehensive Plan.” The Rank I Comprehensive Plan’s Rural Area policy expressly favors “alternatives to urbanization” (Packet Pg. 508). The 1993 North Valley Area Plan directs the County to “limit access to Second Street, Alameda Boulevard, and Montaño Road” and retain residential zoning along these arterials. The Applicant testified the site was chosen to serve the greater Albuquerque metro and west-of-river community — a regional draw, not a local-serving use.

Additionally, this CPC finding was being rewritten in real time during deliberations when the Chair called for a vote; no final text was read aloud before the Board acted. Commissioner Leon Guerrero stated he needed more information before voting. Commissioner Trujillo stated he needed more time. The vote proceeded anyway.

Not supported — contradicts the County’s own Comprehensive Plan; CPC finding not finalized at time of vote
Brief Section: IV.H, IV.I, IV.Q
ZA Finding 6 / CPC Finding 7 “applicant intends to provide” parking, buffers, fencing
What Was Found

“The applicant intends to provide off-street parking, landscaping, buffer landscaping and fencing in compliance with the requirements of the Comprehensive Zoning Ordinance of Bernalillo County... There will be a six-foot-tall wall along the north side.”

What the Record Shows

The NOD relies on what the Applicant “intends” to provide. That is not proof of compliance or a substitute for enforceable conditions addressing the broader impacts. At least two conflicting site plans exist on buffer width — the original showing a buffer narrower than required, and a revised plan submitted the day before the hearing. No final, stamped landscape plan confirming the buffer width and wall specifications was entered into the record in a form the public or appellant could review before the vote. The finding uses future-tense “intends to provide” rather than confirming actual compliance.

Not supported — no verified, stamped plan confirms compliance; “intends” is not proof
Brief Section: IV.L
ZA Finding 7 / CPC Finding 8 “Friday service could be moved”
What Was Found

“The applicant also stated that times of the main Friday service could be moved to accommodate traffic patterns.”

What the Record Shows

This was an applicant statement, not a binding condition. The approval imposed no required service time, peak-hour limit, attendance limit, vehicle limit, or mechanism to enforce the claimed mitigation. The Applicant’s testimony about moving Friday service was offered as evidence that traffic impacts would be manageable — but nothing in the conditions makes it enforceable.

Not supported — applicant’s unenforceable statement cannot substitute for the required traffic finding
Brief Section: IV.G
ZA Finding 9 / CPC Finding 11 “TIA required at building permit phase”
What Was Found

“Staff from public works stated that a Traffic Impact Study would be required at the building permit phase of the project. Road improvements to offset any impacts from the project could be required.”

What the Record Shows

This does not support approval; it confirms that the material traffic analysis had not yet occurred. The filed traffic analysis argues that a future building-permit study cannot replace the pre-approval evidence needed for the CUP safety finding. A future TIA cannot retroactively supply the substantial evidence Section 24 requires at the CUP decision point.

Not supported — a future study is an admission that analysis has not occurred; cannot supply pre-approval evidence
Brief Section: IV.A, IV.F, IV.G, IV.N
ZA Finding 10 / CPC Finding 13 “conditions protect public health, safety, welfare”
What Was Found

“Conditions of approval are necessary to ensure orderly development of the site, as well as the protection of the public health, safety, and general welfare in conformity with the intended purpose of the Zoning Ordinance.”

What the Record Shows

At the time this finding was adopted, the record contained no grading and drainage plan, no hydrology report, no noise-mitigation plan (other than no outdoor speakers), no fire safety or emergency access plan, and no stormwater pollution prevention plan. The Appellant calculated that a 1-inch rain event generates approximately 70,000-85,000 gallons of runoff (corrected per Amendment and Errata), with no detention plan anywhere in the record. Every one of these safety analyses was deferred to the building-permit stage — a non-public process that cannot supply the substantial evidence Section 24 requires at the CUP decision point.

The same defect applies to traffic safety. Condition 9 defers the Traffic Impact Study to the building-permit stage — the same future-promise problem the brief identifies for drainage. The Board could not find that conditions protect public welfare when the single most consequential safety analysis — whether a 350-600 worshipper facility on a documented failing corridor generates undue traffic hazards — had not been conducted. A deferred TIA is an admission that traffic safety was not established at the time of approval, not evidence that it was.

The ZA’s NOD imposed THREE conditions (construction permits + building footprint, impervious surface coordination, and no outdoor amplified sound). The CPC carried forward all three and expanded to 15 conditions, but most of the additions merely restate existing code requirements (parking per Section 21, landscaping per Section 19, lighting per Light Pollution Ordinance, signage per Section 12, noise per Noise Ordinance). The conditions do not impose a pre-operation traffic study, enforceable traffic mitigation, emergency-access requirements, operational limits, drainage analysis, lighting controls, or attendance limits.

Not supported — safety plans Section 24 identifies did not exist at the time of the vote; conditions are mostly restatements of existing code, not additional protections
Brief Section: IV.M (runoff figure corrected in the Amendment and Errata)
CPC Finding 14 only “site is within the Rural Area of the Comprehensive Plan”
What Was Found

“The site is within the Rural Area of the Comprehensive Plan.”

What the Record Shows

This finding is factually correct — the site IS in the Rural Area. But the Commission used it as a neutral descriptor rather than applying the Rural Area policy. The Rank I Comprehensive Plan’s Rural Area policy favors “alternatives to urbanization” (Packet Pg. 508). A 60,000-SF high-volume regional draw facility is the definition of urban-scale encroachment into a designated rural area. The Commission identified the rural designation without analyzing whether a complex of this scale is compatible with it.

Legally insufficient — factually correct but fails to apply the protective policy
Brief Section: IV.Q

Not supported — condition contradicts the record; no stamped drawings reflect the removal

CPC Condition 6 “height is limited to 26 feet”
What Was Conditioned

“The building shall meet all setback, separation, height, and area requirements of the A-1 zone... Height is limited to 26 feet without exception.”

The Applicant’s agent testified the first floor is 43,000 SF and the second floor is 15,000-17,000 SF, but no elevation drawings exist in the record to verify compliance.

Brief Section: IV.F, IV.G, IV.N

Additional Evidentiary Gaps

Documented in the brief but not tied to a single NOD finding:

  • No building height in feet anywhere in the record — not in the application, staff report, site plan notes, or NOD. Height and floor area measure fundamentally different things. (Brief § IV.I)
  • Applicant’s trip count shifted throughout testimony — “100 to 180 attendees” then “140 to 150 cars” then “100 to 120 cars at peak” then “maximum capacity of 600 worshippers.” Even the lowest figures exceed the 100 peak-hour-trip TIA threshold. (Brief § IV.G)
  • Population transfer never quantified — the Applicant testified existing mosques would close and congregations transfer; no figures or analysis provided. (Brief § IV.E)
  • Balloon Fiesta concerns never addressed — AIBF’s June 25, 2026 letter independently identified the absence of a traffic study; no TIA, no seasonal analysis, no enforceable conditions were produced. (Brief § IV.P)
  • Public Works Technical Standards Section 8.4 violated — no traffic scoping report was completed; no PWTR number was activated. (Brief § IV.N)
  • RLUIPA was misapplied — Director Hamm’s warning that requiring a TIA could expose the County to RLUIPA liability was legally incorrect; RLUIPA does not exempt religious institutions from neutral traffic standards. (Brief § IV.D)
  • Unanswered continuance motions — Appellant’s timely 60-day and 120-day continuance motions were never ruled upon; the hearing proceeded as if they had never been filed. (Brief § IV.C)
  • Extraordinary majority vote requirement — the 20% protest petition was filed with over 65% of qualifying acreage within 100 feet; the BCC must apply the extraordinary-majority standard. (Brief § IV.R)

Bottom Line

The County could accurately establish the identity of the application, the zoning category, the site’s Rural Area location, and that other permits would still be needed. But those are not the findings that carry the approval.

The appeal’s central position is therefore sound: the County did not have adequate record evidence to support the findings that actually justify CUP approval — particularly the traffic finding (ZA Finding 3 / CPC Finding 4), site adequacy (ZA Finding 2 / CPC Finding 3), neighborhood compatibility (ZA Finding 4 / CPC Finding 5), compliance intentions (ZA Finding 6 / CPC Finding 7), the unenforceable Friday service assurance (ZA Finding 7 / CPC Finding 8), the deferred TIA (ZA Finding 9 / CPC Finding 11), and the welfare-protecting conditions (ZA Finding 10 / CPC Finding 13).

The ZA explicitly found “no undue traffic congestion or hazards” without a TIA — an unsupported finding. The CPC then dropped that finding entirely, replacing it with a narrow ingress/egress observation and a TIA deferral — failing to make the required Section 24 traffic finding at all. Either way, the record lacks the substantial evidence Section 24 requires.

The CPC added conditions (school removal, 60,000 SF cap, 26-foot height) that appear nowhere on any stamped drawing in the record — and the ZA had imposed only one condition. The conditions that supposedly protect public welfare mostly restate existing code rather than imposing actual additional protections.

Whether that requires outright reversal or a remand for proper findings is for the deciding body, but the later Traffic Impact Study cannot retroactively supply the pre-approval evidence the NOD relied on.
